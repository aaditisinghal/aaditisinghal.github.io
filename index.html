May – Aug 2026

India

Reakon cofounder & CEO

Under India’s GST rules, a business can only reclaim the tax on a purchase if the supplier filed that invoice correctly. Suppliers file late, file wrong, or don’t file at all, and owners quietly lose the credit. Reakon pulled a company’s GST returns from the government, checked every purchase invoice against what suppliers actually filed, and told the owner on WhatsApp what they could claim, what was at risk, and which vendor to chase.

turned down Infosys InStep and JPMorgan for summer ’26 to build this

I started on the ground: more than 90 in-person interviews with chartered accountants and business owners in Delhi, Gurgaon and Bangalore. That became a network of 250+ CAs and 175+ business owners, and it’s where the six verdicts the product gave each invoice came from.

  • Every headline number was checked by automated invariants before each deploy and again every day in production.
  • A sync layer that skipped businesses who were already up to date cut calls to the rate-limited government data provider by about 90%.
  • I found a bug that showed tax credits that didn’t exist and fixed it before it turned into a five-figure misstatement.
  • 6+ businesses used it in production, and we were accepted into the AWS, Google for Startups and Claude for Startups programs.

Made with Next.js, TypeScript, Supabase, Twilio for WhatsApp and Gemini.

Aug 2025 – Apr 2026

Atlanta

ProfitWise cofounder & CEO

A bank feed says ACH CREDIT SP NORTHWIND - WHOLESALE 4,182.55. The ledger has an open invoice for Northwind Oat Bars at 4,200.00. Nothing links the two. ProfitWise was our answer for small businesses: connect the bank, the books, the payment processors and invoice email, and it works out which deposit pays which invoice, shows its reasoning, and only asks a person when the evidence is weak.

We took it from idea to a shipped product through Georgia Tech’s CREATE-X Startup Launch and ran pilots with founders from Georgia Tech and Atlanta Tech Village.

  • Matching runs as a five-stage waterfall. Cheap, certain rules go first, and only the leftovers reach an LLM, scored by a seven-factor confidence model so AI costs stay low.
  • 14 connectors (Plaid, QuickBooks, Xero, Stripe, Shopify, Gmail and more) feed one data layer, so adding a source never touches the matching logic.
  • CI with tests, linting and secret scanning on every change; 200+ production releases.

Made with Next.js, React, TypeScript, Postgres, Redis and Google Cloud.

Quota

Winner, Elastic track

A commissions desk run by agents. It works out what each sales rep has earned, catches mistakes in the sales records, and backs every payout with evidence when a rep disputes it. I built it around one rule: models read the words, code decides the dollars. Every payout needs a person’s sign-off and lands in an append-only, hash-chained ledger that refuses any write that breaks an invariant.

On a held-out test with 40 planted errors of 16 kinds, it caught all 40, with zero false flags and zero wrong payouts. Elastic semantic search found 10 of 12 off-contract promises hidden in emails, against 5 of 12 for keyword search.

first held-out run was 39/40. the miss was a bug in my answer key, not in Quota

Made with Python, FastAPI, React, Elastic, a Stripe sandbox and Claude.

MHacks

Homie

1st place, Fetch.ai Agent Challenge, and Best Use of Notability

Renting in the US assumes you’re already here. Leasing offices close before students abroad wake up, they won’t call international numbers back, and without an SSN you’re rejected automatically. Homie is eleven agents on Fetch.ai that take one text and do the rest: read listing sites, call leasing offices in parallel with an ElevenLabs voice, play the offers against each other, and start the application in a live browser. In a live run it held the best deal 156 seconds after the first message.

I rebuilt the agent messaging as a request-ID RPC layer so 10 calls and 6 site crawls could run at once without collisions, and turned the voice path into a zero-transcoding μ-law bridge from Twilio to ElevenLabs, which brought call response time down to about a second. The agents say they’re AI on every call and never submit a real application without you.

Made with Python, Fetch.ai uAgents, Twilio and ElevenLabs.

HackHERS

PreggoPillow

1st place overall, and MLH Best Use of Presage

A smart pregnancy pillow and a phone web app. The pillow streams readings overnight, and the app learns your baby’s own movement pattern and tells you the night it changes. It never says a baby is fine, it isn’t a medical device, and emergency or reduced-movement messages never go to the language model. They get fixed scripts instead.

A Go service takes in the readings and runs the baseline and anomaly detection on TimescaleDB, with a Next.js app on top. Doctor’s documents sit in an encrypted vault, and you can call an AI assistant by voice.

Made with Go, Next.js, TypeScript, Tiger Data (TimescaleDB), Gemini and ElevenLabs.

YC hackathon

Unicess

3rd place, Primitive track

A student who emails three university offices gets three partial replies, weeks apart. Unicess gives each office an email agent with its own address. A student writes once, every office the question touches answers in parallel, and the student gets one complete, signed reply. The median time to first reply in a live run was 6.1 seconds. If the sender is the student’s own AI assistant, it gets back a JSON block it can act on.

Anything the agents can’t answer goes to a staff review queue, and the answer is learned for next time.

Made with TypeScript, Node and Primitive. 42 unit tests.

2026

Mapi

Memory for AI agents that knows when a fact has gone stale. If a team moved from Jenkins to CircleCI to GitHub Actions, most memory APIs return all three. Mapi stores memories as a graph with “supersedes” and “contradicts” edges, hides anything a newer result replaces, and can tell you what it believed on a given date.

Retrieval fuses vector and keyword search by rank, then reranks and drops near-duplicates. It scores 82.4% on LongMemEval’s 500 questions with an independent judge.

Made with Python. 1,320 tests, strict typing.

HackMIT

EcoAI

1st place, Infosys Diamond track

Rewrites LLM prompts to use fewer tokens without changing what they ask, leaves code blocks, URLs and template variables alone, and keeps a receipt of the tokens, cost and estimated emissions saved. After HackMIT we rebuilt it properly, with real authentication, a real test suite and a Python SDK.

Then we checked the carbon math on real hardware: an NVIDIA L4 running Qwen2.5-1.5B under vLLM, with Zeus reading actual GPU power. Measured energy barely moved, because output tokens dominate a request’s energy and a shorter prompt only shrinks the first pass. The token savings are real. The energy claim is narrower than our dashboard implied, and the write-up says so.

the GPU test didn’t back our headline number. we published it anyway

Made with Python, Flask, vLLM and Zeus.

2025

HackHarvard

HivePath AI

Most Loved Project

Route optimizers minimize distance, so when a fleet can’t serve every stop, they drop the hard-to-reach ones first: no kerbside parking, stairs only, a blocked loading bay. HivePath makes those stops more expensive to skip. It scores each stop’s accessibility from Street View imagery and feeds that into an OR-Tools solver with time windows and capacity limits.

Across 59 synthetic scenarios with the fleet capped at 72% of demand, hard-to-reach stops were served every time with the weighting on, against about 75% without it, at the same overall throughput. Routes also came out about 27% shorter than serving stops in the order they were submitted.

Made with Python, OR-Tools and FastAPI. 190 tests.

Mercor hackathon

Imaced

3rd place (and a team dinner)

We asked a vision-language model which side of a mammogram had a mass. It said left, correctly, with 94% confidence. Then we covered the mass with a gray box and asked again. It said right, with 71% confidence. Imaced sits after the model and decides, question by question, whether to pass the answer along or send it to a person, and says why.

It reads the model’s own answer probabilities across reordered options, a version of the question with no image, and an added “cannot be determined” choice, then combines them with a small logistic regression. On held-out questions it sent 30% of the should-defer cases to review, against 20% for confidence alone, and the answers it let through were right 90% of the time.

Made with Python, Qwen2.5-VL-7B and the ViTAR-16K dataset.

Research

Dec 2024 – now

research lead

Machine unlearning

How do you honor a “right to erasure” request when the data is already baked into a model’s weights? I lead a project designing selective-forgetting methods (influence functions, parameter isolation and Fisher-based approaches) and benchmarking them on healthcare and finance data. The best so far forget the requested data with at most a 3% drop in accuracy. We’re also building reproducible measures of how well a model has actually forgotten, toward a journal paper.
